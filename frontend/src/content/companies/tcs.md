Tata Consultancy Services (TCS) hires more freshers than almost any other employer in India, and nearly all of that hiring runs through one exam: the **TCS National Qualifier Test (NQT)**. Your NQT score decides whether you are shortlisted and which role you are considered for. This guide explains the process from registration to joining, and what to prepare for each stage.

## How TCS fresher hiring works

TCS runs the NQT several times a year, both for students in their final year and for recent graduates. The process has four stages:

1. **Register** on the TCS NextStep portal and apply for the NQT drive.
2. **Take the NQT**, an online test held at TCS iON centres or remotely, depending on the drive.
3. **Interview**: candidates who clear the cut-off get a combined technical, managerial and HR interview.
4. **Offer and onboarding**: selected candidates receive an offer for a specific role and later a joining date.

The NQT score is valid for a fixed period (CHECK: commonly two years), so a good score can be reused for later TCS openings.

## Who can apply

Eligibility is strict and checked again before joining, so read it carefully before you register.

| Requirement | Usual rule (CHECK for your drive) |
|---|---|
| Degree | BE, BTech, ME, MTech, MCA or MSc (some drives also accept BCA, BSc and other graduates for specific roles) |
| Year of passing | The batches named in the drive notification, usually the final-year batch and one or two previous years |
| Marks | 60% or 6 CGPA in Class 10, Class 12 (or diploma) and graduation |
| Backlogs | No pending backlogs at the time of joining (CHECK the allowed number at the time of the test) |
| Education gap | Usually no more than 24 months, with a valid reason |

If you don't meet a requirement, don't register hoping it will be overlooked. Documents are verified before joining and offers can be withdrawn.

## The roles: Ninja, Digital and Prime

TCS offers different roles depending on your NQT score and interview performance. The names have stayed the same for several years, but the packages are revised, so treat the figures below as indicative.

| Role | Who it suits | Indicative package (CHECK) |
|---|---|---|
| Ninja | Clears the foundation sections | Around ₹3.4–3.6 lakh per year |
| Digital | Strong advanced section and coding | Around ₹7 lakh per year |
| Prime | Top scorers who also do well in a tougher interview | Around ₹9–11.5 lakh per year |

You don't choose your role up front. You take one test, and the result decides which interview track you are invited to.

## NQT test pattern

The NQT has two parts. Everyone takes the foundation section; the advanced section is what opens the Digital and Prime tracks.

**Foundation section** (CHECK timings and question counts)

- **Numerical ability**: percentages, ratios, time and work, time and distance, profit and loss, probability, data interpretation.
- **Verbal ability**: reading comprehension, sentence completion, error spotting, para-jumbles.
- **Reasoning ability**: series, coding-decoding, blood relations, seating arrangements, syllogisms.

**Advanced section** (CHECK)

- **Advanced quantitative and reasoning ability**: harder versions of the foundation topics.
- **Advanced coding**: usually two programming problems. You can write the solution in common languages such as C, C++, Java or Python. Hidden test cases check edge cases, so a solution that only passes the sample input scores poorly.

There is no negative marking in recent NQT drives (CHECK), so attempt every question.

## The interview

Candidates who clear the cut-off are invited to an interview, often held online. It usually combines three panels in one sitting:

- **Technical**: your final-year project, one programming language in depth, OOP concepts, DBMS and SQL queries, basic operating systems and networking. For Digital and Prime, expect a coding problem and deeper questions on data structures.
- **Managerial**: how you handle pressure, teamwork, and situational questions such as a disagreement with a teammate or a missed deadline.
- **HR**: relocation (TCS can place you in any city), willingness to work in shifts, the service agreement, and document checks.

Be ready to explain everything on your resume. Interviewers commonly pick a project or skill from it and ask you to walk them through it.

## How to prepare

A realistic plan for four to six weeks before the test:

- **Weeks 1–2: aptitude.** Practise one topic a day from the numerical and reasoning lists above, with a timer. Speed matters as much as accuracy.
- **Weeks 2–4: coding.** Solve two or three easy-to-medium problems a day on arrays, strings, sorting, searching and basic maths. Always test your solution against edge cases: empty input, one element, very large numbers.
- **Weeks 4–5: verbal and mock tests.** Take at least three full-length timed mocks. Review every wrong answer.
- **Week 6: interview.** Revise your project, OOP, DBMS and SQL. Practise explaining your project in two minutes.

Our [interview preparation guide](/career-guide/interview-tips) covers the technical and HR rounds in more detail, and the [resume builder](/career-guide/resume-builder) helps you prepare a resume that matches what interviewers ask about.

## Common mistakes

- **Registering with wrong details.** Your name, marks and year of passing must match your documents exactly.
- **Skipping the advanced section.** Even if you're aiming for Ninja, a good advanced score can move you to a higher track.
- **Hard-coding outputs in the coding round.** Hidden test cases catch this, and it can count against you.
- **Paying agents.** Register yourself on the official TCS NextStep portal; nobody can "book" you a seat or a result. TCS does not charge for interviews, offer letters or joining, so any request for money at those stages is a scam.
